What a daily reading can and cannot tell you

A transit is a conversation between the sky as it is now and the sky as it was when you were born. The Moon crosses your natal Venus; Mars squares your Midheaven. Nothing is being done to you. A transit describes the weather you are making decisions inside of, which is genuinely useful, and it does not describe the decision. That part stays yours.

The reading is built in two halves, because a day has two halves. First the collective sky: the Moon's sign, whatever is retrograde, and the one or two aspects among the planets that everyone alive is under today. Then your own contacts: the transits that touch your specific chart, on the clock, in the order they arrive. The second half is the part a sun sign horoscope cannot give you, because it does not know where your Venus is.

On the matter of time: the Moon moves roughly thirteen degrees a day, which is fast enough that the minute matters and slow enough that you can feel the hour turn. The outer planets are different. Pluto can sit within a degree of your Sun for the better part of a year. Giving that a time to the minute would be theatre, so this tool marks it as background instead: real, present, and not on a clock. Some days have five timed contacts and some have none. Both are honest answers, and a day with nothing exact is a day to work from your own structure rather than the sky's mood.

If you know your birth time, use it. Your Ascendant and Midheaven move about a degree every four minutes, so they cannot be guessed, and they are often where the most useful transits land: how you are coming across, and what is happening to your work. Without a time you still get a true reading of your planets. You just get it without the angles.

Common questions

How is this different from a sun sign horoscope?

A sun sign horoscope gives the same twelve readings to everyone alive. This one is computed against your own birth chart: the transiting Moon and planets are measured to your natal Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, and, if you know your birth time, your Ascendant and Midheaven. Two people born the same day in different hours get different readings.

Why do some transits have an exact time and others say "in orb"?

The Moon moves about thirteen degrees a day, so its aspects perfect at a specific minute and that minute is worth knowing. Jupiter through Pluto can hold the same aspect for weeks, so there is no honest minute to give. Those are shown as background, in orb, rather than dressed up with false precision.

Do I need my exact birth time?

No, but it changes what you get. Without a time, your Moon is approximate and your Ascendant and Midheaven cannot be calculated at all, so no transit to your angles can be reported. With a time, the reading includes contacts to how you meet the world and to your career and public role, which are often the most useful lines of the day.

Can I read a past or future date?

Yes, any date within five years either way. The sky is computed fresh for whatever date you pick, so a day last March and a day next autumn are both real readings rather than recycled text.

Is my birth data stored?

No. Birth details are used in memory to compute the chart and then discarded; they are never stored or logged on the server. If you tick "remember my details", they are saved only in your own browser, and you can clear them at any time.
