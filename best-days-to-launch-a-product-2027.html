How these dates are chosen

A launch is about being seen, so it leans on the Sun (light, visibility) and Jupiter (reach, goodwill). The Moon should be waxing, so the thing grows after it ships, not void of course (or it 'goes nowhere'), and applying to a benefic, with the Sun ideally on an angle so it stands in the light. The dates above favor a Moon clear of Mars and Saturn.

What to avoid

A void-of-course Moon is the classic mistake for a launch: the old rule is that nothing done under it comes to much. If your launch also involves signing or heavy messaging, keep Mercury direct (avoid Feb 9 to Mar 3, Jun 10 to Jul 4, Oct 7 to 28). The eclipse seasons around Feb 6 and 20 and around Jul 18, Aug 2, and Aug 17 are dramatic, unpredictable ground for a debut.

Why does the void-of-course Moon matter for a launch?

The tradition holds that ventures begun under a void Moon tend to fizzle. Every date here keeps the Moon out of the void at the peak window.
